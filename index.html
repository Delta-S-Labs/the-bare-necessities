01

A cocktail bar,
not a chain.

A McDonald's serves millions and knows none of them. That's fine — for McDonald's. But it means every dish is designed for the average person, who doesn't actually exist.

We do the opposite. Each app we launch is a small, deliberate room. We work closely with the people inside it. They ask for things. They give feedback, raise feature requests — and we actually ship them. When the room fills, we don't cram in more tables. We open another bar.

The AI era is what makes this finally possible: for the first time, you can genuinely serve a specialized request for a power user at software scale. So we do.

02

Meet Baloo

The first bar we opened. An AI concierge for the everyday hunger — food, groceries, a table for two — named for the bear who taught the jungle to look for the bare necessities.

You talk to Baloo like a person who has your back. “Order dinner — something light, I had a heavy lunch.” It knows your diet, your budget, your weekly calorie cap, and it just… handles it.

  • Orders food Reads the room and picks something that fits your day — placed and paid, over Swiggy.
  • Restocks the kitchen Groceries topped up on Instamart before you notice you're out.
  • Books the table “Book us a table for Saturday too” — held, confirmed, done.
  • Runs your week Recurring plans — weekday lunches, Tue/Thu dinners, a Sunday restock — on a schedule you can flip on and off.

Screens show a seeded demo account — no real orders were placed.

03

We put a cap
on the guest list.

Growth, for us, isn't a bigger crowd — it's a better one. Each app opens to a small number of members and grows slowly, in deliberate steps. When we reach the cap, the doors don't burst open. A seat frees up only when someone leaves.

If the room is full, you join the queue — and take a seat the moment one opens. Fewer people, served properly, beats everyone served the same.
